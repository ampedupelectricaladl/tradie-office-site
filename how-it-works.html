The install, day by day

  1. Day 1

    Kick-off call, 45 minutes

    We go through how enquiries reach you now, what you charge a callout at, your hours, and the jobs you don't want. We record it so you only say it once.

  2. Days 2 to 3

    We connect the systems

    ServiceM8, your CRM and phone number, Gmail, Google Calendar, your Google review link, and Telegram for your approvals. You click approve on the connections. We do the wiring.

  3. Days 4 to 5

    We write your assistant

    We draft how it talks, using your words from the kick-off call. You name it. Ellie is the default. Plenty of people rename it.

  4. Days 6 to 7

    Quote packs built

    We build your quote PDF template off your real ServiceM8 lines, and the spec sheets for the products you install most. No prices go in the spec sheets.

  5. Days 8 to 9

    Dry run

    Everything runs against real enquiries with sending switched off. You read what it would have said. We change what you don't like.

  6. Day 10

    You approve the wording

    Sit down for half an hour and read the drafts. This is the meeting that matters. If a sentence isn't how you'd say it, we change it.

  7. Days 11 to 12

    Go live, with you in the loop

    Sending switches on. Every single message waits for your approval. You'll get your first approval email and your first Telegram summary.

  8. Day 13

    Walkthrough, 30 minutes

    How to approve, how to reject, how to change a line, how to turn it off. Recorded so you can watch it again.

  9. Day 14

    Handover and the clock starts

    You get the written handover. The 30-day guarantee starts the day you go live.

A day in the life, once it's running

6:45am — the digest

One email. What came in overnight, what's waiting on you, what's booked today. You read it in the van before you start.

9:10am — you miss a call

You're in a ceiling. Thirty seconds later the caller gets a text back asking what the job is and where. They reply with a photo of a scorched switchboard.

9:40am — a form comes in

A new enquiry from your website. It gets an answer with the questions you'd have asked. You don't touch your phone.

12:30pm — smoko, five minutes of approvals

Telegram tells you four things are waiting. You open the approval email and tap through. Three yes. One you reject because you know that street and you're not doing it.

2:15pm — a quote goes out

You price the switchboard job in ServiceM8. The pack builds itself: quote PDF, switchboard spec sheet, photos of the last three boards you did. You approve. It sends, then texts them that it landed.

4:00pm — an old quote gets chased

A quote from last Tuesday hits day 3. It checks the texts and the emails first. She replied "leave it with me" on Friday, so it waits. The other one gets a polite nudge.

5:30pm — a booking

Someone says Thursday works. It goes into ServiceM8 and your calendar, and a confirmation goes out. The reminder is already queued for Wednesday.

6:10pm — you finish a job

You mark it complete. An hour later the customer gets one short, personal review ask with a direct link. One ask. No nagging.

8:00pm — the quiet bit

An enquiry lands at 8pm. It gets answered. A text that would land after 7pm gets held until morning, because nobody wants a business text at 9:30 at night.

Total time you spent on office work: about fifteen minutes.

The approval bit, properly explained

This is the part people ask about most, so here it is in full.

Everything the assistant writes goes into a queue. Once a day you get one email listing every item, with the exact words that would be sent and the reason it wants to send them. You approve or reject each one. A Telegram message tells you it's waiting.

Before an item even reaches you, it has to pass a set of checks. No dollar figure in any message. No promise that you'll call at a certain time. No claim about stock or a delivery date. No claim that a booking exists when it doesn't. If a check fails, the message doesn't go in the queue at all.

Once one of those paths has run 20 times without you changing a word, you can choose to let that path run hands-off. Review asks are usually the first one people let go. Anything involving money never goes hands-off. That's not a setting.
